# The Meta Behavioral Interview

The Meta behavioral round is one 45-minute conversation in the full loop about how you work, not what you know. Older guides call it the Jedi round. Interviewers are reported to compare your past behavior with Meta values such as Move Fast, Be Bold, and Focus on Impact.

The round carries real weight from E5 upward, where it also supplies evidence for the level. A candidate with strong code and weak stories is commonly reported to receive a lower level or no offer.

## How Meta frames the round

Meta does not publish a list of principles for interviews in the way that some companies do. Preparation guides report the following values and what each one sounds like in a question.

| Value | What the interviewer listens for | Example question |
|---|---|---|
| Focus on impact | You chose work by its value to users or the business, and you measured the result | Tell me about something you built that improved a process or an experience |
| Move fast | You delivered in steps and changed direction when facts changed | How do you handle requirements or deadlines that change quickly? |
| Be bold | You took a reasoned risk or proposed a new idea | Tell me about a time you took a risk on a project |
| Be open | You share information and give and receive direct feedback | How do you like to give and receive feedback? |
| Long-term thinking | You planned for growth beyond the first release | Describe a decision that cost time now and saved time later |

## What is assessed

| Area | Evidence the interviewer wants |
|---|---|
| Collaboration | Work with other engineers, product managers, and designers toward one goal |
| Conflict | A disagreement you handled directly and without damage to the relationship |
| Ownership and initiative | Work you started or led without being asked |
| Adaptability | A change of plan that you absorbed and explained to others |
| Growth | A failure or piece of feedback, and what you changed afterward |
| Motivation | A specific reason for choosing Meta and the role |

## Question themes commonly reported

- Tell me about something you have done that you are proud of.
- Tell me about a recent project and your part in it.
- How have you shown leadership when you were not the manager?
- Describe a time you worked with a difficult team member.
- Tell me about a significant challenge at work and how you handled it.
- How do you prioritize when several deadlines compete?
- What is your favorite Meta product, and how would you improve it?

More questions are in the [question bank](question-bank.md).

## How to prepare stories

STAR is a four-part structure for a behavioral answer: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

| Part | Content | Share of the answer |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | The context in two sentences | 10 percent |
| Task | Your own responsibility | 10 percent |
| Action | The steps you took, in the first person | 60 percent |
| Result | The measured outcome and what you learned | 20 percent |

Follow these steps before the loop.

1. Write six to eight stories from the last three to five years. Cover a proud project, a conflict, a failure, a change of direction, leadership without authority, and feedback.
2. Give each story a number in its result, such as a latency reduction or a count of users affected.
3. Label each story with the one or two Meta values it shows.
4. Set the scope for the target level: end-to-end ownership for E5, and influence across teams for E6.
5. Practice each story aloud in two minutes, and prepare answers to three follow-up questions.
6. Use one Meta product for a week and prepare one specific improvement to it.

## Signals that weaken an answer

- "We" in every sentence, which hides your own contribution.
- A failure story with no failure in it.
- A result with no number.
