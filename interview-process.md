# The Meta Interview Process

The Meta software engineer process has six stages: application, recruiter conversation, technical screen, full loop, review with team match, and offer. Meta is reported to describe a typical process of about two to three months. Each stage answers one question about the candidate, and this page lists them in order.

The details below are commonly reported by candidates and preparation guides. Meta does not publish a rubric or exact round lengths, so confirm the format with your recruiter.

## The stages in order

| Stage | Length | Format | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Application and resume review | One to two weeks | A recruiter compares the resume with the role | Whether a recruiter contacts you |
| 2. Recruiter conversation | 20 to 30 minutes | Phone or video; experience, projects, motivation; no coding | Whether you enter the process, and the target level |
| 3. Technical screen | 45 minutes, one or two sessions | One or two coding questions in CoderPad, a shared online editor | Whether you are invited to the full loop |
| 4. Full loop | 4 or 5 interviews of about 45 minutes | Coding, design, and behavioral rounds, on site or on video | The evidence for the hire decision and the level |
| 5. Review and team match | One to two weeks, then two weeks or more | Written feedback is reviewed; you meet managers with open roles | Hire or no hire, the level, and the team |
| 6. Offer | A few days | The recruiter prepares and presents the offer | The final package |

## What the full loop contains

The full loop is the set of final interviews, often called the onsite. Older guides use three internal names for its rounds, and recruiters sometimes still use them.

| Round | Older name | Count | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coding | Ninja | 2 | One or two problems in 45 minutes, with clear reasoning and tested code |
| Design | Pirate | 1 or 2 | System design or product architecture; entry-level candidates usually skip it |
| Behavioral | Jedi | 1 | Motivation, work with other people, conflict, and fit with Meta values |

Senior candidates sometimes report one more conversation about career goals and leadership experience. Some roles add a round on a specific domain, such as frontend development or machine learning.

## Facts worth knowing before you start

- **The target level is set early.** The recruiter names a target level, and the loop is scored against that level. Ask for it in the first call. See [levels](levels.md).
- **The design round has two types.** Ask the recruiter whether your design round is system design or product architecture. See the [system design interview](system-design-interview.md).
- **The screen is a coding round.** The [coding interview](coding-interview.md) page covers the pace, which is the main reason candidates fail the screen.
- **Some rounds may include an AI assistant.** Meta is reported to say that select roles now have an approved AI assistant inside CoderPad. Ask whether your round includes it.
- **Every candidate is reported to hear back.** Meta says the recruiter reports the result with or without an offer. Detailed feedback for each round is not promised.
- **Team match comes after the decision.** A hire decision is followed by conversations with managers, and this step adds time to the process.

## A reasonable timeline

| Step | Commonly reported wait |
|---|---|
| Application to recruiter reply | One to two weeks |
| Recruiter conversation to technical screen | About one week |
| Technical screen to result | About one week |
| Full loop to result | One to two weeks |
| Result to team match | Two weeks or more |
| Team match to offer | A few days |

These waits describe the usual pattern and are not confirmed by Meta. After a round, wait about one week before writing to the recruiter.
